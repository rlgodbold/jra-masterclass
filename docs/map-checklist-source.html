Part 1  ·  Foundation

Do these once and get them exactly right. Everything else sits on top of them.

  • Verify the profile.Do exactly what Google's verification screen asks for, whether that is a video walkthrough or live prompts. Do not overthink it. Follow the screen and keep going until you are verified.
  • Set your primary category, and get it exactly right.This is the biggest single switch on the whole profile. Set it to your core business, which for most of you is Junk removal service. Then add every honest secondary category for work you actually do: garbage collection, dumpster rental, demolition. The wrong primary category makes you invisible for the searches that matter, and nothing else you do will fix it.
  • List every service, and write a real description for each one.Furniture removal, appliance haul off, hot tub removal, mattress disposal, estate cleanouts, hoarding cleanup, garage and basement, construction debris, commercial, storage unit cleanouts. Specific beats generic. Google's AI matches somebody asking who can haul out a hot tub to the profile that says it removes hot tubs.
  • Make your name, address and phone match everywhere.Character for character, on your website and everywhere else you are listed.
  • Fill in your hours. Claim 24/7 only if you truly answer.Complete hours signal a real, open business. If you answer around the clock, say so. If you do not, do not claim it.
  • Show a real address if you have one.Hiding your address and running service area only costs you pack rank, even for a company that works out of a truck. If you have an address you actually operate from, show it.
  • Link your website, and tag the link.Point the profile at your site with a tracking tag so the leads Google sends you show up in your analytics. Something like yoursite.com/?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp. Then when somebody asks whether the map is actually working, you have a number instead of an opinion.
  • Stand up the Map Engine.A website whose only job is to rank the profile. Every page mirrors a category or a service and links back to the profile: a home page, one page per service, one page per town you serve, then pricing, reviews, about and FAQ.

Part 2  ·  The weekly rhythm

This is the part almost nobody does, and it is the part that actually moves you up.

  • Post real job photos, near daily, and post them in batches.Before and after shots from actual jobs. Every crew carries a phone and every job makes a before and an after, so this costs nothing but the habit. Our own data: junk removal companies that post at least once a week, and post several photos rather than one, see 41% more verified phone calls and online bookings. Both halves matter. One photo a week is not it, and thirty photos once a quarter is not it either. Google publishes no photo schedule; this is what we measure across the profiles we manage.
  • Put up one Google Post a week.Posts do not lift rank directly, but they convert people already looking at you and they feed Google's AI current content. Schedule them so it is not a weekly decision.
  • Run the review ask on every completed job, out loud, on site.At job completion, face to face, while the empty garage is right there in front of them. Use the exact words in the box below and have the crew memorize them.
  • Always text from your own company number.A personal text from the number they already have saved converts far better than a blast from a review app shortcode they do not recognize.
  • Run the four touch sequence, then stop.Text immediately. Email the next morning. Text again the day after that. One final text five days later. Then let it go. Four touches across about a week, across two channels. Chasing them past that costs you the goodwill you just earned and the referral behind it.
  • Respond to every review, good and bad.A short, human reply on all of them. A calm answer to a bad review sells better than a wall of fives, because it shows how you handle a problem.
“Would you mind sharing how we did and what part of the area your job was? Please be as descriptive as possible to help future potential customers.”

That one question does three jobs. It gets the service quality in their own words, it gets the neighborhood, which feeds Google's local matching, and it gives them a reason to write more than four words, because you are asking them to help the next person rather than to help you. Never write it for them. The whole point is that it is theirs.

Aim for a 4.7 to 4.9 average rather than a spotless 5.0. A perfect record with hundreds of reviews reads as curated, and it can convert worse than a few honest four star reviews sitting alongside the rest.

Where to ask, and where never to

Do not rotate where you ask. Ask everybody for Google. Nearly every customer already has a Google account through an Android phone, Gmail or Chrome, and Google is the only platform that feeds the map pack. Never trade a Google review for a review somewhere else.

Diversify by where the job came from, not by rotation. If the lead came from Angi, ask for the Angi review. Thumbtack, ask Thumbtack. Nextdoor, ask Nextdoor. They definitely have an account, because they used it to find you, and the review compounds the channel that sent you the job. Both Angi and Nextdoor tell pros to ask.

Never ask for a Yelp review. Yelp's policy bars asking anyone at all, including friends and family. Soliciting can get you demoted in Yelp search and a public Consumer Alert banner on your page, and Yelp filters solicited reviews into the not recommended tab where they do not count anyway. Handle Yelp passively instead: claim the page, fill it out completely, and put the Yelp badge in your website footer and email signature so Yelp users find you on their own.

Never lose a review to the wrong platform. The ask is the question, not the platform. If they do not have a Google account, say whatever is easiest for you, Google, Facebook, wherever you already have one. Facebook is the universal fallback, and a review somewhere beats no review anywhere.

If you use a review link page, put Google first with the alternatives underneath, and route by which platform they prefer. Never route by star rating. That is gating, and it breaks both Google's policy and FTC rules.

A review a week beats 50 reviews from two years ago.

Part 3  ·  Every month

One sitting a month. This is where you find out whether any of it is working.

  • Run a geo-grid scan and write down your percentage.A geo-grid drops pins across your service area and checks where you land in the map pack at each one. Your number is the percentage of the grid where you are in the top three. It is the only ranking number worth tracking, because your rank differs in every neighborhood.
  • Work your weakest cells.The neighborhoods where you are not in the top three are your to do list: more jobs there, photos taken there, a city page for that town.
  • Count the reviews on the company sitting at number three.Beat that count. You do not need the most reviews in the world. You need to clear the floor in your market.
  • Count that same company's local links, then close the gap and stop.Links are the cheapest gap to close in a weak market, not the engine. Do not go chasing link volume.
  • Do one real local link action.Join a chamber, more than one inside about 70 miles if your market is competitive. Sponsor a local team, charity or event. Get on a local podcast, blog or news story. One a month is plenty.
  • Publish one new city or service page on the Map Engine.Keep widening the coverage that feeds the profile.

Part 4  ·  Never do these

Google removed 13 million fake Business Profiles and blocked 292 million policy-violating reviews in 2025. It audits realness at a scale it never could before, so being unmistakably real is now the winning move rather than the boring one.

  • Never stuff keywords into your business name.Best Cheap Junk Removal Austin is not your name. It is a violation, and any competitor can report it.
  • Never gate, buy or incentivize reviews.No ten dollars off for five stars. No sending happy customers to Google and unhappy ones to a private form. That is an FTC problem on top of a Google problem.
  • Never use a fake or virtual address.No mailbox store, no address you do not actually operate from.
  • Never buy cheap bulk link packages.They are a liability, not a lever.

Part 5  ·  Earn the right to expand

  • Report the cheaters above you.If a competitor ranked above you is stuffing keywords into their name or running a fake listing, report it through Google's Business Redressal Complaint form. Getting a spammer removed from above you is the fastest free rank gain there is.
  • Do not open a second profile until you have earned it.All three of these have to be true first.
  • Your primary profile is pulling 15 or more reviews a month on its own.
  • You are prepared for a new location to take about 18 months to mature.
  • You will never funnel reviews from your primary to the new location. That drains the profile that was working and you lose the thing that got you here.

Expansion is something you earn with a humming primary, not a shortcut around building one.

Start here  ·  your first week

If the list above feels like a lot, do these five things in the next seven days. They are the ones that pay first.

  • Open your profile and check your primary category today.It takes two minutes and it is the biggest lever in this entire document. If it is wrong, nothing else you do matters much.
  • Show your address if you have one, and fill in your hours.Two more settings, both one time, both worth real pack rank.
  • Take photos on every job this week and post them in batches.You do not need a plan for this. Before and after, every job, several at a time.
  • Teach the crew the review ask, word for word.Then run the four touch sequence on the very next completed job. One job is enough to prove the system to yourself.
  • Run one geo-grid scan and write the number down.You cannot tell whether any of this worked without a starting number. Get it this week, before you change anything else.
