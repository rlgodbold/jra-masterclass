The LSA Migration
Checklist

Built for junk removal owners.
Lee Godbold  ·  Junk Removal Authority

Google is shutting down the old Local Services Ads dashboard. Your account moves into Google Ads on its own, and junk removal is on the list for August 2026.

You get an email about 14 days before your account moves, then a reminder at 7 days, then it happens. You do not pick the date.

One thing only works before that day. Your customer records come with you: names, phone numbers, message threads, even old call recordings. Your performance history does not. Once the old dashboard closes, what you spent and what it earned you is gone for good.

Your customer list comes with you. Your scoreboard does not.

Accurate as of August 2026. This is still rolling out, so check the notice in your own account.

Part 1  ·  The migration checklist

Work it in order. Only the first group has a deadline attached to it.

Before your notice arrives
  • Capture your performance history. Plan on taking screenshots. We have not found a download button for the performance reports, so plan on doing this by hand. In the Reports section, take three passes: 1. One screenshot of all of 2025.
    2. One screenshot of this year so far, January 1 through today.
    3. One screenshot of every single month, back to January 2025. One month at a time. It is tedious, and it is the only version that lets you compare this April to last April.     Grab leads, spend, the call versus message split, and the breakdown by service. Put it all in a named folder in your Drive, not your downloads folder. Then hand the screenshots to an AI and have it build you a spreadsheet. If you are a JRA client and your LSA is connected to your Google Ads account, we have software that pulls this for you. If we find an easier way, we will publish it.
  • Screenshot your settings. Budget, your bid, services, service area, hours, phone routing, photos, callouts, and the list of who has access and at what level. You cannot check that something transferred correctly if you never wrote down what it was.
  • Turn off any service you do not actually do. Google no longer refunds a lead just because it was for a service you do not offer, or from an area you do not cover. Every service left switched on is a bill you already agreed to pay. Part 2 shows exactly which ones to have on.
  • Make sure the account email is one you read every day. If your notice goes to an old address, or to somebody who left, you burn the 14 days without knowing it.
  • Give every person who handles leads their own Google Ads login. Your lead inbox is moving into Google Ads. One shared login will not carry your office person through. Sort it out now, not on migration day.
  • Write down what a lead really costs you and how often you close one. You are about to set a target and let Google aim at it. Better that number comes from your own books than from a guess.
  • Clean up your Google Business Profile. Name, phone, hours, service area, photos. Those details now feed your paid ad, not just your map listing. Careful: a big change to your name or address triggers a review that can pause your ads for a day or two, so do not do it the week you expect to move.
  • If an agency runs your LSAs, email them this week. Ask for the performance export, and ask what your access looks like after the move. Get it in writing.
When the notice arrives
  • Read the notice itself. It is the only thing that knows your actual date. Nobody outside Google can tell you.
  • Capture your performance one more time, so the last few weeks are in there.
  • Take a fresh set of settings screenshots, dated.
  • Tell your team the lead inbox is about to move, and have each of them test their own login. On their own phone. Do not assume it works.
  • Decide your target cost per lead now, calmly, instead of on the day.
  • Block thirty minutes on your calendar for migration day. That is when you check everything, while you still remember what it looked like before.
On migration day
  • Check every setting against your screenshots. Budget, target, services, locations, hours, phone routing, photos, callouts. Fix what did not carry over.
  • Check the budget math. Your old weekly budget gets divided by 7 to make a daily number. That daily number times 30.4 is the most you can spend in a month. That works out to about 4.34 times your old weekly budget, not 4 times. Look at it before it surprises you.
  • Find your leads. They are under Goals, then Conversions, then Leads. Your old contact details, message history and call recordings are in there too.
  • Confirm every lead handler can open it, on their own login.
  • Pick your callouts. The Better Business Bureau callout is gone. Choose replacements from what your account offers, things like your hours, your specialties, and how you take payment.
The first two weeks after
  • Leave it alone and let it settle. Give it two weeks before you judge anything. It takes that long for the system to find its footing, and early numbers will lie to you.
  • If you need to slow down, lower the budget. Do not pause. Pausing sends it back to square one and you pay for that twice. Our rule: never move your budget or your target more than 20 percent at a time.
  • After that, check spend once a week for the first month. Watch your pacing and your answer rate together. Junk removal is spiky, weekends and weather and end of month moves, and you no longer have a weekly budget smoothing that out. If you run dry by noon, you are invisible for the rest of the day.

Part 2  ·  Getting the most out of LSA

This part is not about the migration. It is how you keep your cost down and your leads good, before and after the move.

Turn on the right services
Junk Removal Provider, four of ten switched on. This is the screen, under Profile and budget.
Have these onGeneral junk removal  ·  Furniture removal  ·  House and garage cleanout  ·  Construction waste removal
If you do any of these you can test them, but we generally see low returnAppliance removal  ·  Donation pickup  ·  Electronic waste  ·  Hazardous waste  ·  Yard waste  ·  Other

Those four are the ones we recommend every junk removal company run. You can try the others if you genuinely do that work, just watch what those leads cost you and turn them back off if they do not pay.

Why this matters more than it used to. Google no longer gives you money back for a lead that came in for a service you do not offer, or from an area you do not serve. Those used to be your two easiest refunds. Choosing your services carefully is now the main way you control what you spend.

Keep your service area tight

Go a little wider than you normally would, because when Google is not sure exactly where the caller is, it leans toward companies that cover more ground. Then stop. Trucks that stay in a tight area get seen more and drive less. Widen it when you have the trucks, the people and the routing to handle it, and not before.

Answer the phone, fast, every time

Every LSA call runs through a Google number, so Google knows whether you picked up and how long you talked. There is no published target to hit. You are being measured against the other companies in your market, which means this is the one thing you can always beat them on. A missed call costs you twice: you lose the job, and you lose position for the next one.

If a machine answers for you, it has to actually do the job. It needs to hold a real conversation and book the work, or hand the call straight to a person. An answering service that takes a message does not count.

How we set it up. During business hours, let it ring three times to your team. If nobody picks up by then, forward the call to an AI like Jenny, which answers immediately.

Some marketers will tell you to answer faster than that. We would rather a real person get there first, because it is simply better service. The AI is your backup, and it means the call never goes unanswered.

If you are doing everything else right and your impression share still is not where you want it, shorten the ring count.

Rate every bad lead

You can no longer file a dispute. Google reviews leads on its own now and credits some of them back automatically, and the rating survey on the lead is how you tell it what went wrong. Credits show up in your balance within about 30 days, and the original charge still shows on the invoice, which is why people think it never happened.

Your reviews live in your Business Profile now

Since July 2025, every LSA review is managed through your Google Business Profile, and the old LSA review links no longer work. If your review request texts still point to one, fix them today. One review engine now feeds both your map ranking and your ads.

Add photos every month

Your customer is looking at three ads side by side and picking one. Real photos of your trucks and crew do that convincing, and you can add them to your ad without touching your Business Profile. Google publishes no schedule for this. Monthly is simply what we do, because a profile nobody has touched in two years looks like a business nobody is running.
